Debriefing

The study examines whether support for giving power to one leader changes when public services recover from a crisis. For some assigned sets of participants, each fund point produced the same group return under both decision methods in Block 2. For others, a fund point continued to produce a larger group return when a leader decided.

The leader's decision rule remained unchanged in both conditions: the leader could require the same amount from all five citizens and could move fund points to a personal account. The study therefore examines whether participants return to individual citizen choice after the leader's higher return per fund point disappears, even though the leader can still coordinate the group's allocations.

The leader's ability to move public-service points to a personal account gives that authority a personally consequential use. The study examines both whether citizens permit that authority and what selected leaders do with it. No single choice is interpreted as evidence that a participant is corrupt or opposed to democracy.

Changing a choice is not treated as inconsistency. The study is designed to observe how people update decisions when conditions change.
