Study title: Political institutions and public-service provision in a changing environment

Researcher: Hector Bahamonde, Project Researcher
Contact: hector.bahamonde@utu.fi

Purpose and procedures

This study investigates how people respond to public-service performance and make group decisions. The study takes place in one laboratory session lasting approximately 75--90 minutes.

You will complete two blocks of ten decision rounds in anonymous five-person groups. You will choose between two ways of making decisions, decide how experimental points are used, and see the resulting payoffs. Groups are reshuffled anonymously across rounds within a fixed set of participants. In some rounds one participant may be selected to make a decision for the group.

Payments

You receive a participation payment of {{ participation_fee }} plus performance-based earnings. Each experimental point is worth {{ conversion }}. One decision round from each block is selected randomly for payment, so every round can affect your earnings.

Risks and benefits

The foreseeable risks are minimal. Some public-service scenarios or decisions about unequal allocations may be mildly uncomfortable. You may stop participating at any time. There is no direct personal benefit, although your participation may improve scientific understanding of group decision making.

Privacy, withdrawal, and attendance

No unnecessary identifying information is collected in the experimental interface. Results may be published or presented, but individual participants will not be identified. Participation is voluntary. You may withdraw without penalty, although performance payments require completing the relevant tasks.

Because decisions are interactive, arriving on time and remaining for the full session are important. Other participants' progress can depend on everyone in their assigned set being present.
