Study title: Political institutions and public-service provision in a changing environment

Researcher: Hector Bahamonde, Project Researcher
Contact: hector.bahamonde@utu.fi

Purpose and procedures

This study investigates how people learn from public-service performance and make group decisions. The study has two laboratory sessions, scheduled for {{ wave1_date_display }} and {{ wave2_date_display }}, each lasting approximately 55--65 minutes.

In each session you will complete ten decision rounds in anonymous five-person groups. You will vote between two ways of making decisions, make public-budget choices, and see the resulting payoffs. Groups are reshuffled anonymously across rounds within a fixed set of participants. In some rounds one participant may be selected as the group leader.

Payments

You receive a participation payment of {{ participation_fee }} plus performance-based earnings. Each experimental point is worth {{ conversion }}. One decision round from each session is selected randomly for payment, so every round can affect your earnings. A completion bonus is also paid according to the instructions.

Risks and benefits

The foreseeable risks are minimal. Some political scenarios or decisions about unequal allocations may be mildly uncomfortable. You may stop participating at any time. There is no direct personal benefit, although your participation may improve scientific understanding of institutional choice and democratic resilience.

Privacy, withdrawal, and attendance

No unnecessary identifying information is collected in the experimental interface. Results may be published or presented, but individual participants will not be identified. Participation is voluntary. You may withdraw without penalty, although performance payments and the two-session completion bonus require completing the relevant tasks.

Because decisions are interactive, arriving on time for both scheduled sessions is important. Other participants' progress can depend on everyone in their assigned set being present.
