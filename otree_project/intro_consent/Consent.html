Study: Group decisions and public-service provision in a changing environment

Principal Investigator: Janne Tukiainen, Professor, Turku School of Economics, University of Turku
Contact: janne.tukiainen@utu.fi, +358 50 308 3620

Purpose and procedures

This study investigates how people respond to public-service performance and make group decisions. It takes place in one laboratory session lasting approximately 60 minutes.

You will complete two blocks of ten decision rounds in anonymous five-person groups. You will choose whether a selected person's proposal requires group approval, make decisions about experimental points, and see the resulting payoffs. Groups are reshuffled anonymously across rounds within a fixed set of participants.

Payments

You receive a {{ participation_fee }} participation payment plus a performance bonus of up to {{ bonus_cap }}. One round from each block is selected randomly, and the points you earn in those rounds determine the bonus. Every round can therefore affect your earnings.

Risks and benefits

The foreseeable risks are minimal. The fictional public-service situation or decisions about unequal allocations may be mildly uncomfortable. There is no direct personal benefit beyond payment, although your participation may improve scientific understanding of group decision making.

Privacy and voluntary participation

The experimental interface does not collect unnecessary identifying information. PCRC retains the identifying information needed for recruitment and payment and gives researchers only coded responses. PCRC deletes the identity-to-participant-code link after payment. Coded experimental data are stored on University of Turku IT servers in Finland and may be retained for compatible future scientific research. Results may be published or presented, but individual participants will not be identified.

Participation is voluntary. If you do not wish to participate, please tell the laboratory manager before entering the interactive task.

Because decisions are interactive, arriving on time and remaining for the full session are important. Other participants' progress can depend on everyone in their assigned set being present.
