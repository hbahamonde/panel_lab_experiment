Imagine that you live in a society where public services have been under serious strain for a long time. Parliament has failed to pass a budget for eight months, healthcare waiting times have doubled, and planning for public services has stalled.

During the experiment, you and the other participants are the citizens of this society. You will make decisions about a public-services fund. Points you keep count toward your payoff. Points that remain in the fund create group points that are shared equally among the five citizens.

What happens in the experiment?

There are 20 paid rounds, divided into two blocks of 10. At the start of each round, you receive 20 points and join four other participants. Each group member chooses whether a selected person's proposal will require group approval or take effect directly. The method chosen by at least three group members is used for that round.

Only you can see the option you select. After the group chooses a method, the software selects one of the five citizens at random. Everyone has the same one-in-five chance in every round. That person proposes the same fund allocation for everyone and may propose moving some fund points to their own payoff.

When group approval is required, the other four citizens see the proposal. At least three must approve it. If they reject it, everyone chooses their own fund allocation. When a decision takes effect directly, the proposal is implemented without this approval vote. Everyone sees the result and their own payoff.

The screen will always show the current fund rates before you choose. A rate may change between the two blocks.

There is no automatic time limit on your decisions. Please answer promptly. The laboratory manager monitors progress and will assist if a participant is delayed.

Who is in your group?

You remain in the same matching pool of 10 or 15 participants throughout the experiment. After every round, the software reshuffles the pool into new five-person groups. Identities are never shown.

How are you paid?

After all 20 rounds, the computer randomly selects one round from each block. The points earned in those two rounds determine a performance bonus of up to {{ bonus_cap }}, and you also receive the {{ participation_fee }} participation payment. More points produce a larger bonus. Because any round may be selected, make every decision as if it will count.

The next screens explain both methods. You will practice making a proposal, deciding whether to approve one, and choosing an individual allocation after rejection. Practice choices are not paid.
