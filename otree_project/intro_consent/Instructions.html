Imagine that you live in a society where public services have been under serious strain for a long time. Parliament has failed to pass a budget for eight months, healthcare waiting times have doubled, and planning for public services has stalled.

During the experiment, you and the other participants are the citizens of this society. You will make decisions about a public-services fund. Points you keep count toward your payoff. Points that remain in the fund create group points that are shared equally among the five citizens.

What happens in the experiment?

There are 15 paid rounds: 10 in the first block, followed by five in the second. At the start of each round, you receive 20 points and join four other participants. Each group member chooses whether a selected person's proposal will require group approval or take effect directly. The method chosen by at least three group members is used for that round.

Other participants cannot see the option you select. After the group chooses a method, the software selects one of the five citizens at random. Everyone has the same one-in-five chance in every round. That person proposes the same fund allocation for everyone and may propose moving some fund points to their own payoff.

When group approval is required, the other four citizens see the proposal. At least three must approve it. A 2–2 split means rejection. If they reject it, everyone chooses their own fund allocation. When a decision takes effect directly, the proposal is implemented without this approval vote. Everyone sees the result and their own payoff.

The screen will always show the current fund rates before you choose. A rate may change between the two blocks.

On decision screens, a 90-second countdown is a guide to keep the session moving. Choose your answer, then click Next to send it. When the countdown reaches zero, a reminder appears and the page stays open. The researcher or trained research assistant monitors progress and can help if you are delayed.

Who is in your group?

You remain in the same matching pool of 5, 10, or 15 participants throughout the experiment. In a five-person pool, you play with the same four people in every round. In a larger pool, the software reshuffles participants into five-person groups before each round. The software does not show names or identities.

How are you paid?

One round from each block is selected at random for payment. Each of the 10 rounds in the first block has the same chance, as does each of the five rounds in the second. The selected rounds are revealed after all 15 rounds. Your points from these two rounds are converted at a rate of 24 points = €1. They determine a performance bonus of up to {{ bonus_cap }}, and you also receive the {{ participation_fee }} participation payment. More points produce a larger bonus. Because any round may be selected, make every decision as if it will count. At the end, the screen will show the code you use to collect your payment from the research team.

The next screens explain both methods. You will practice making a proposal, deciding whether to approve one, and choosing an individual allocation after rejection. Practice choices are not paid.
