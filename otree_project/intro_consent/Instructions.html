Imagine that you live in a society where public services have been under serious strain for a long time. Parliament has failed to pass a budget for eight months, healthcare waiting times have doubled, and planning for public services has stalled.

During the experiment, you and the other participants are the citizens of this society. You will make decisions about a public-services fund. Points you keep count toward your payoff. Points that remain in the fund create group points that are shared equally among the five citizens.

What happens in the experiment?

There are 20 paid rounds, divided into two blocks of 10. At the start of each round, you receive 20 points and join four other citizens. Each of you chooses one of two ways to make the decision: Citizens decide or A leader decides. The method chosen by at least three citizens is used for that round.

Only you can see the option you select. After all five citizens have chosen, everyone sees how many selected each method and which method the group will use. You then make the relevant decision and see the result and your payoff.

The screen will always show the current fund rates before you choose. A rate may change between the two blocks.

Who is in your group?

You remain in the same set of 10 participants throughout the experiment. After every round, the software reshuffles these 10 people into new five-person groups. Identities are never shown.

How are you paid?

After all 20 rounds, the computer randomly selects one round from each block. Your payoffs from those two rounds determine your performance payment. Each point is worth {{ conversion }}, and you also receive the {{ participation_fee }} participation payment. Because any round may be selected, make every decision as if it will count.

The next screens explain the two decision methods. You will try both methods before the paid rounds begin. Practice choices are not paid.
