Debriefing

During the task, the two methods were described as Group approval required and Decision takes effect directly. The study examines whether people use a group ballot to remove approval over a later binding decision and whether they subsequently vote to restore that approval.

For some matching pools, public-service conditions improved and the two fund rates became equal in Block 2. For other pools, the crisis continued and fund points under decisions implemented without approval continued to use the higher rate. Under both methods, the selected person could propose the same allocation and a personal transfer; what differed was whether the other citizens could reject the proposal.

The study therefore examines whether participants vote to restore group approval after its crisis-based productivity disadvantage disappears. No single choice is interpreted as evidence that a participant is corrupt, authoritarian, or opposed to democracy.

Changing a choice is not treated as inconsistency. The study examines how institutional choices respond when public-service conditions and their material consequences change.
