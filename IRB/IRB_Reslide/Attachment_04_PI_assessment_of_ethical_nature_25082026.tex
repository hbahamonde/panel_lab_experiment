\IRBTitle{Attachment 4}{Principal Investigator's Assessment of the Ethical Nature of the Research}

\small

\sectionbar{Assessment}
The study targets 300 adults who complete one paid, approximately 60-minute laboratory session. Participants make anonymous, incentivised decisions in a fictional public-service setting. The study does not use deception about the rules or payment calculation, collect biological samples, intervene in physical integrity, expose participants to unusually strong stimuli, or target children or other groups whose capacity to consent is impaired.

Foreseeable burdens are limited to waiting in a synchronized task and possible mild discomfort with a crisis scenario or unequal point allocations. Registration through the host laboratory's participant system provides its general rules and privacy information. Before the experimental task, participants receive project-specific plain-language information and give a short electronic confirmation that they voluntarily agree to this study. They may decline or stop without giving a reason or suffering negative consequences. The laboratory manager finalises a consenting roster divisible by five before paid play, monitors the session, and manages interruptions without generating decisions on a participant's behalf.

The task is scientifically necessary because the research question concerns consequential choices, repeated interaction, and whether the same participant changes an institutional decision after conditions change. The design minimises burden through one visit, unpaid practice, comprehension checks, neutral wording, and payment of EUR~10 plus a performance bonus of up to EUR~5.

\sectionbar{Privacy and data protection}
The production oTree study and coded research data are hosted on University of Turku IT servers in Finland, irrespective of the host laboratory. The host laboratory retains the identifying information needed for recruitment and payment and gives the research team only coded data. The research team never receives any temporary identity-to-code link; the host laboratory removes it after compensation under its procedure. Access to coded research data is restricted to Janne Tukiainen, Hector Bahamonde, and authorised personnel. Results are reported without identifying participants. Coded data may be retained for verification and compatible future scientific research as described in the privacy notice and data management plan.

The final questionnaire directly asks about past party vote, left--right placement, and attitudes toward democracy and political authority; these answers are handled as special-category personal data. Data minimisation, optional responses, separation of identities, restricted access, secure University storage, disclosure review, and aggregate reporting reduce the residual risk. The accompanying project-specific DPIA documents the assessment and will be reviewed if the platform or collected variables change materially.

\sectionbar{Conclusion}
In my assessment, the protocol's scientific value is proportionate to its limited foreseeable risks and burdens. The participant information, consent procedure, compensation, laboratory safeguards, and data-protection measures support voluntary and informed participation. Data collection will begin only after the required ethical and institutional approvals are in place.
