\IRBTitle{Attachment 11}{Privacy Notice for Scientific Research}

You are participating in a scientific study at the University of Turku. This notice explains how your personal data are processed in the study \emph{\StudyTitle} under the EU General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR).

\sectionbar{1. Data controller and research contact}
\textbf{Data controller:} University of Turku, FI-20014 University of Turku, Finland; telephone +358 29 450 5000.

\textbf{Responsible researcher and contact for research-related matters:} Janne Tukiainen, Professor, Turku School of Economics, University of Turku; \StudyEmail; \StudyPhone.

\sectionbar{2. Data Protection Officer}
University of Turku Data Protection Officer: \href{mailto:dpo@utu.fi}{dpo@utu.fi}; telephone +358 29 450 3009.

\sectionbar{3. Research and purpose of processing}
The study examines how 300 completed adult participants make consequential group decisions about a public-services fund and whether choices over group approval change after a fictional public-service crisis either improves or persists. Participants remain in matching pools of 10 or 15 and are reshuffled into five-person groups. Personal data are processed to recruit and compensate participants, run the interactive task, preserve the randomized design, analyse decisions and questionnaire responses, verify results, and report scientific findings.

\sectionbar{4. Persons processing the data}
Janne Tukiainen, Hector Bahamonde, and authorised personnel may process coded research data. PCRC laboratory personnel process identifying scheduling, attendance, and payment information and the temporary identity-to-code link. University IT personnel may administer the servers without using the data for research. External collaborators receive only anonymised data unless their role and lawful access are documented before disclosure.

\sectionbar{5. Duration of processing}
Personal data are processed from recruitment through data collection, planned for 1--31 October 2026, and subsequent analysis and reporting. PCRC deletes the identity-to-code link when compensation has been paid. Separate payment and accounting records are retained only as required by administrative and legal obligations. Coded research data without direct identifiers are retained for verification and compatible future scientific research. Their continued necessity is reviewed five years after publication of the primary results and regularly thereafter; data no longer needed for an approved scientific purpose are securely deleted or irreversibly anonymised.

\sectionbar{6. Lawful basis}
Processing is based on a task carried out in the public interest for scientific research: GDPR Article 6(1)(e) and section 4(3) of the Finnish Data Protection Act (1050/2018). Acceptance of TOKA's general rules and the study-specific ethical consent to participate are documented separately; neither is used as the lawful basis for personal-data processing.

\sectionbar{7. Personal data included}
The research dataset may contain:
\begin{itemize}
  \item participant, session, matching-pool, pool-size, and temporary-group codes;
  \item the study-specific consent response, form version, and timestamp;
  \item randomized condition, method ballots, leader selection, proposals, transfers, approval votes, allocations, contributions, group outcomes, and payoffs;
  \item timestamps, response durations, delay flags, practice and comprehension responses;
  \item unpaid expectations and post-task questionnaire responses about approval and limits on a leader's authority; and
  \item limited technical log information required for security and troubleshooting.
\end{itemize}
Names, contact details, and information required for recruitment or payment are held by PCRC, are not entered into the experimental interface, and are kept separately from research responses. Researchers receive only coded experimental data.

\sectionbar{8. Special categories of personal data}
The final questionnaire asks about approval rights and limits on political authority. These answers may reveal political opinions and are therefore treated conservatively as special-category personal data. Their processing is necessary for scientific research in the public interest under GDPR Article 9(2)(j) and section 6(1)(7) of the Finnish Data Protection Act, subject to GDPR Article 89 safeguards. The study does not ask for party affiliation, voting history, health, ethnicity, religion, sexual orientation, biometric data, or criminal records.

\sectionbar{9. Sources of personal data}
Research responses are obtained directly from participants through oTree during the laboratory session. PCRC obtains scheduling and payment information directly from participants through its normal administrative processes and assigns the session code. The study does not link experimental responses to public registers or external commercial datasets.

\sectionbar{10. Protection measures and direct identifiers}
The interface collects research data without direct identifiers and uses participant codes. PCRC keeps the temporary identity-to-code link separately and deletes it once payment is complete; the research team never receives it. The production oTree application and coded data are hosted on University of Turku IT servers with an \texttt{utu.fi} domain in Finland. Institutional credentials, access control, encrypted connections, managed backups, logging, and two-factor authentication where available protect the data. Results are reported only in aggregate or de-identified form.

\sectionbar{11. Transfers and recipients}
Personal data are not transferred outside the University of Turku as controller. PCRC and University IT process only the information required for their operational roles. The public prototype is not used for live research data.

\sectionbar{12. Transfers outside the EU or EEA}
Personal data are not transferred outside Finland or outside the European Union or European Economic Area.

\sectionbar{13. Processing after the research}
Coded research data are retained without direct identifiers on University IT-provided storage to assess reliability and for compatible future scientific research in accordance with GDPR Article 5(1)(b) and (e). The need for retention is reviewed five years after publication of the primary results and regularly thereafter. A new privacy notice will be provided for a new use when required; if participants can no longer be identified, the notice will be made available through the University's website. Anonymised data, documentation, analysis code, and non-identifying materials may be retained and shared for reproducibility and future scientific use.

\sectionbar{14. Your rights}
Depending on the circumstances and lawful basis, you may have the right to access, correct, or erase your data, restrict or object to processing, and not be subject to consequential automated decision-making. Data portability does not apply to processing based on a public-interest task. Any research-related limitation is assessed case by case and used only when permitted by law and necessary to avoid preventing or seriously impairing the research. Contact Janne Tukiainen to exercise a right.

Before payment, PCRC may be able to locate a record using its separate link. Once PCRC deletes that link, the research team cannot identify which coded record is yours. GDPR Article 11 then applies unless you provide additional information that reliably identifies the record. The study does not use automated decision making or profiling that produces legal or similarly significant effects.

\sectionbar{15. Right to complain}
You may lodge a complaint with the Finnish Data Protection Ombudsman's Office if you believe your personal data have been processed unlawfully: Lintulahdenkuja 4, 00530 Helsinki; PO Box 800, 00531 Helsinki; telephone +358 29 566 6700; \href{mailto:tietosuoja@om.fi}{tietosuoja@om.fi}.
